# B0064 · Turning ambiguity into a concrete plan

| Section | Topic | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| Behavioural, Role & JPMorganChase | Behavioral - ambiguity | Medium |

## Question

Tell me about a time you turned an ambiguous requirement into a concrete plan.

## Answer

- Situation: "Build an agent for the risk team" with no defined scope.
- Action: interviewed users and observed their workflows, ranked the top three tasks by time spent, defined success metrics and non-goals, wrote a one-page phased proposal, built a thin slice to validate it, and got sign-off.
- Result: a clear roadmap, with phase one delivered in six weeks and measurable time savings.

The signal: you reduce ambiguity through discovery and writing, not by waiting for perfect requirements.

## Likely follow-ups

- What did you deliberately leave out?
- How did you handle requirements changing later?
